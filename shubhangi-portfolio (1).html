Turning a Recurring Engineering Bottleneck into a Self-Service Platform

Self-service configuration platform · B2B SaaS ATS

~200+ eng. hrs/mo saved +

Context

A multi-tenant B2B SaaS Applicant Tracking Platform served 13+ staffing agencies, each requiring frequent configuration changes — client-specific values, PDF templates, email, notification, and SMS templates.

Problem

Every configuration change required an engineering ticket. Agencies were blocked for days waiting on routine changes engineering considered low-value work.

Why It Mattered

Engineering time was spent on repetitive configuration work instead of core platform development, while agencies faced slow, unpredictable turnaround for basic operational needs — a growing source of account friction as the client base scaled.

My Role

I drove this initiative end to end—from identifying the recurring pattern across configuration requests and defining the product requirements to working with engineering on the solution and shaping the admin-facing configuration experience.

Constraints

Engineering capacity was limited and shared across initiatives. Any solution had to be safe enough for non-technical agency admins to self-serve without introducing data-integrity or cross-tenant risk.

Approach

Instead of treating recurring configuration requests as separate features, I identified the underlying pattern and proposed a unified platform approach rather than isolated fixes.

Solution

A 5-channel self-service configuration platform — valid values, PDF templates, email templates, notification templates, and SMS templates — letting agency admins independently create, customize, and manage configurations without engineering involvement.

Key Decisions & Trade-offs

I weighed a shared configuration data model against shipping the first channel faster in isolation, prioritizing the shared model despite slower initial delivery. Validation logic was split between admin-controlled configuration and engineering-controlled guardrails, to avoid trading an engineering bottleneck for a support-ticket bottleneck instead.

Learnings

The biggest lever wasn't the UI — it was recognizing that five "small" tickets were actually one system-level problem. I now look for the shared root cause before scoping any request as an isolated feature.

Outcome: Eliminated an estimated 50+ engineering requests/month, saved an estimated 200+ engineering hours/month, and cut configuration turnaround from multi-day engineering cycles to same-day self-service across 13+ agencies.

Migrating 15,000+ Records Without Breaking Trust in the Data

Self-service candidate migration · Data integrity at scale

~90% effort reduction +

Context

New agencies onboarding onto the platform needed to migrate existing candidate records at scale, often from inconsistent legacy formats.

Problem

Manual, ad hoc candidate data migration was slow, error-prone, and created a serious onboarding bottleneck — directly delaying how quickly a new agency could go live.

Why It Mattered

Data integrity issues discovered after go-live were expensive to fix and eroded a new agency's early trust in the platform — exactly when trust mattered most.

My Role

I owned the end-to-end product definition for a self-service candidate migration capability, including validation logic, error-handling behavior, and the admin-facing migration experience.

Constraints

Legacy data was inconsistent and often incomplete. The solution needed to handle bad data gracefully rather than assume clean input, without requiring engineering involvement per migration.

Approach

I designed for imperfect input rather than assuming clean data—using automated validation, clear error handling, and data-integrity controls to help users identify and resolve issues during migration.

Solution

A self-service candidate migration capability supporting automated validation, error handling, and data-integrity controls at scale.

Key Decisions & Trade-offs

A key design principle was transparent error handling—making migration issues visible and actionable rather than allowing errors to go unnoticed.

Learnings

Reliability isn't just about correctness — it's about how a system fails, and whether the user can trust what it tells them when it does.

Outcome: Enabled staffing teams to migrate 15,000+ candidate records at scale, reduced migration effort by an estimated ~90%, and removed a key bottleneck in new-client activation.

Choosing a Scalable Partnership Over a Stalled Integration

Marketplace distribution strategy · Sourcing & reach

25K+ job boards unlocked +

Context

Staffing agencies needed broader access to job-distribution boards to source candidates effectively.

Problem

A direct integration with a specific job board that clients were requesting stalled on the vendor side, with no reliable timeline to resolution.

Why It Mattered

Without broader job-board access, agencies were limited in candidate sourcing reach — a competitive disadvantage a one-off integration fix wouldn't have solved even if it had shipped.

My Role

I independently investigated the root sourcing gap behind the original request, and drove the evaluation and negotiation of an alternative solution.

Constraints

Engineering resources were already committed elsewhere; any solution needed to avoid repeating the same one-off integration pattern for future job-board requests.

Approach

Rather than continuing to push on the stalled integration, I reframed the underlying need — broad job-board access — and evaluated a marketplace partner that could provide access to a large network of job boards through a single integration.

Solution

A scalable marketplace partnership unlocking access to 25,000+ job boards through one integration, replacing what would otherwise have been repeated one-off vendor builds.

Key Decisions & Trade-offs

I chose a broader, slightly less customized solution over continuing to chase the original client-requested integration — trading short-term specificity for long-term scalability, since most future job-board requests would already be covered.

Learnings

The best product decision isn't always the one the client explicitly asked for — it's the one that solves the underlying need in a way that scales beyond this one request.

Outcome: Unlocked access to 25K+ job boards through a single integration and replaced a reactive, one-off vendor pattern with a scalable distribution strategy.

How I Think About Products

A few principles I keep returning to.

Solve the pattern, not the ticket.

When the same request shows up in different disguises, that's a signal to zoom out — not scope down.

Trust is a feature.

A system that fails silently is worse than one that fails visibly. I design for graceful, transparent failure — not just the happy path.

Ambiguity is where product judgment starts.

The best answer isn't always the one that was asked for. Sometimes the job is reframing the problem before solving it.

Data should compress decisions, not just describe them.

A dashboard that shows everything and decides nothing isn't done yet.

Technical depth changes what's possible — not just what's credible.

Understanding how a system actually works changes which trade-offs you're willing to make, not just how convincing you sound in the room.
